Inventory turnover is cost of sales divided by average inventory. Average inventory is the arithmetic mean of the opening and closing inventory lines.

From the extract, inventory at the beginning of the year:

$$
169
$$

Inventory at the end of the year:

$$
179
$$

Sum the two stock balances:

$$
169 + 179 = 348
$$

Divide by 2 for the average:

$$
\frac{348}{2} = 174
$$

Cost of sales from the extract:

$$
674
$$

$$
\frac{674}{174}
$$

$$
674 \div 174 \approx 3.8736
$$

$$
3.8736 \approx 3.87
$$

Compare with the claimed ceiling of 6.87 times per year:

$$
3.87 < 6.87
$$

Inventory turns about 3.87 times a year, which is below 6.87. The inequality in the claim therefore holds.

So the statement is True.
