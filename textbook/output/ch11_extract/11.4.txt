Chapter 11.4 – Geometric Series (Complete Set: Tasks 1–20)
Tasks 1–20, sorted from easiest to hardest (difficulty 1/5 to 5/5). Key formulas: a finite geometric series with first term
a and quotient k ≠ 1 has sum sn = a + ak + ak2 + ··· + akn−1 = a·(kn − 1)/(k − 1); when k = 1, every term equals a and
sn = a·n. An infinite geometric series a + ak + ak2 + ··· converges if and only if |k| < 1, in which case its sum is a/(1 −
k); if |k| ≥ 1 the infinite series diverges and has no finite sum. Tasks 17 and 20 additionally draw on two related results
about general (not necessarily geometric) infinite series: convergence requires the terms an to tend to 0, though this
is necessary but not sufficient (the harmonic series 1 + 1/2 + 1/3 + ··· is the standard counterexample), and the
p-series rule ∑1/np converges if and only if p > 1. All tasks below rely exclusively on these results. Round dollar and
quantity amounts to 2 decimal places unless otherwise noted.

Task 1. Startup Revenue Growth Over Five Years
Difficulty: 1/5
A newly launched e-commerce startup earns $50 million in revenue this year and expects revenue to grow by
10% annually for each of the next four years, forming a finite geometric series with first term a = 50 (million
dollars) and quotient k = 1.10 over n = 5 years.
Statements (True / False):
a) The expected revenue in year 2 is $55.00 million.
b) The expected revenue in year 5 is approximately $73.21 million.
c) The total revenue expected over the 5-year period is approximately $305.26 million.
d) If revenue had instead remained flat at $50 million per year for 5 years, the total would have been $250
million — exactly $60.00 million less than the actual growth-scenario total.
e) The total revenue expected over the 5-year period is approximately $328.86 million.
Solution – Task 1
Given:
• a (year-1 revenue) = $50 million
• k (annual growth quotient) = 1.10
• n = 5 years
Formula(s):
• sn = a + ak + ak2 + ··· + akn−1 = a·(kn − 1)/(k − 1), for k ≠ 1
• Term in year t (t = 1,…,5): a·kt−1
Step-by-Step Calculations:
Year-2 revenue = a·k = 50×1.10 = $55.00 million.
Year-5 revenue = a·k4 = 50·(1.10)4 = 50 × 1.4641 = $73.205 million → $73.21
million.
k5 = 1.105 = 1.61051.
s5 = 50·(1.61051 − 1)/(1.10 − 1) = 50 × 0.61051/0.10 = 50 × 6.1051 = $305.255
million → $305.26 million.
Flat-revenue total (0% growth, k = 1) = 50 × 5 = $250.00 million.
Difference from actual growth total = 305.255 − 250.00 = $55.26 million (not $60.00
million).
Explanation of Each Statement:
a) TRUE. Year-2 revenue is the year-1 revenue multiplied once by the growth factor: 50×1.10 = $55.00 million,
matching exactly.
b) TRUE. Counting from year 1 (which is simply a itself) up to year 5, the revenue has been multiplied by 1.10 four
separate times — once for each step from year 1 to year 2, year 2 to year 3, and so on up to year 5. So the year-5
revenue equals a·k4 = 50×1.4641 = 73.205, which rounds to $73.21 million.

Task 2. Perpetual Profit Stream from a Subscription Box Line
Difficulty: 1.3/5
A subscription box company just earned $2,000 in profit from its newest product line this month. Because
customer renewals are expected to halve every month indefinitely, monthly profits are modeled as an infinite
geometric series with first term a = $2,000 and quotient k = 0.5.
Statements (True / False):
a) The infinite profit series converges to a finite sum.
b) The infinite sum of all future monthly profits is $4,000.00.
c) The sum of just the first 4 months' profits is $3,750.00.
d) The sum of the first 4 months' profits exceeds the infinite sum.
e) If the quotient were instead k = 1.5, the infinite series would still converge, to a sum of $-4,000.00.
Solution – Task 2
Given:
• a (month-1 profit) = $2,000
• k (monthly decay quotient) = 0.5
Formula(s):
• Infinite sum (|k| < 1): a + ak + ak2 + ··· = a/(1 − k)
• Finite sum of first n terms: sn = a·(1 − kn)/(1 − k)
Step-by-Step Calculations:
|k| = 0.5 < 1, so the series converges.
Infinite sum = 2,000/(1 − 0.5) = 2,000/0.5 = $4,000.00.
First 4 terms: 2,000 + 1,000 + 500 + 250 = $3,750.00.
3,750.00 < 4,000.00, so the partial sum does not exceed the infinite sum.
If k = 1.5, then |k| = 1.5 ≥ 1, so the series diverges and has no finite sum at
all.
Explanation of Each Statement:
a) TRUE. Convergence of an infinite geometric series depends only on the size of the ratio between consecutive
terms, not on any other feature of the series. Here each month's profit is exactly half of the previous month's, so |k|
= 0.5, comfortably below 1 — the running total therefore settles toward a fixed number instead of growing without
bound as more months are added.
b) TRUE. When |k| is less than 1, the sum of every term added together forever, a + ak + ak2 + ···, works out to
a/(1−k). Substituting a=2,000 and k=0.5 gives 2,000/0.5 = $4,000.00, the value the cumulative profit approaches as
more and more months pass.
c) TRUE. The first four monthly profits are 2,000, then 2,000×0.5=1,000, then 2,000×0.25=500, then
2,000×0.125=250. Adding these directly gives 2,000+1,000+500+250 = $3,750.00.

d) FALSE. Every term in this series is a positive dollar amount, so each additional month can only add to the
running total, never subtract from it — the partial sum after any finite number of months must therefore sit below the
full infinite total. Since $3,750.00 (first 4 months) is indeed less than $4,000.00 (all months combined), the first four
months' total falls short of the infinite total by $250.00 rather than exceeding it.
e) FALSE. A ratio of k=1.5 has absolute value 1.5, which is greater than 1, so the successive terms of the series

Task 3. Declining Annual Deposits into a Sinking Fund
Difficulty: 1.6/5
An investor commits to an unusual sinking-fund plan: this year's deposit is $800, and each following year's
deposit is 90% of the previous year's deposit, continuing indefinitely. This is an infinite geometric series with a =
$800 and k = 0.90.
Statements (True / False):
a) The infinite deposit series converges.
b) The infinite sum of all deposits is $8,000.00.
c) The sum of the first 10 deposits is approximately $5,210.57.
d) The first 10 deposits together represent about 65% of the infinite total sum.
e) If the quotient were instead k = 1.10, the series would diverge.
Solution – Task 3
Given:
• a (year-1 deposit) = $800
• k (annual decay quotient) = 0.90
Formula(s):
• Infinite sum (|k| < 1): a/(1 − k)
• Finite sum of first n terms: sn = a·(1 − kn)/(1 − k)
Step-by-Step Calculations:
|k| = 0.90 < 1, so the infinite series converges.
Infinite sum = 800/(1 − 0.90) = 800/0.10 = $8,000.00.
0.9010 = 0.3486784401.
s10 = 800·(1 − 0.3486784401)/0.10 = 800 × 6.513215599 = $5,210.57.
Ratio to infinite sum = 5,210.57/8,000.00 = 0.6513 ≈ 65.13% ≈ 65%.
If k = 1.10, |k| = 1.10 ≥ 1, so the infinite series diverges by definition.
Explanation of Each Statement:
a) TRUE. Since each year's deposit equals 90% of the prior year's deposit, the ratio between consecutive terms is a
constant 0.90, and |0.90| is less than 1. That is exactly the condition needed for the running total of an infinite
geometric series to approach a fixed limiting value instead of growing forever.
b) TRUE. For a convergent infinite geometric series, the total of every term added together equals the first term
divided by (1 minus the ratio). With a=$800 and k=0.90, that gives 800/(1−0.90) = 800/0.10 = $8,000.00 as the
amount the cumulative deposits approach in the long run.
c) TRUE. The sum of the first 10 deposits is given by s10 = a·(1−k10)/(1−k). Since 0.90 raised to the 10th power is
approximately 0.348678, this becomes 800×(1−0.348678)/0.10 = 800×6.513216 ≈ $5,210.57.

e) TRUE. A ratio of k=1.10 has absolute value greater than 1, meaning each deposit would be larger than the one

Task 4. Lithium Reserve Depletion for a Mining Company
Difficulty: 1.9/5
A mining company estimates its lithium reserves at 18,000,000 tons. Current annual extraction is a constant
300,000 tons per year. Separately, analysts also model a scenario in which extraction instead starts at 300,000
tons and grows by 5% per year for 10 years, a finite geometric series with a = 300,000 and k = 1.05.
Statements (True / False):
a) At a constant extraction rate of 300,000 tons/year, the reserves will last exactly 60 years.
b) If extraction were instead held constant at 500,000 tons/year, the reserves would last 36 years.
c) Constant-rate extraction over n years is the special k = 1 case of the geometric series, where the total
simply equals the number of years multiplied by the yearly amount.
d) If extraction instead grows by 5% per year for 10 years, total extraction over those 10 years is
approximately 3,900,000 tons.
e) The 10-year total under 5% annual growth exceeds the 10-year total under constant 300,000-ton
extraction by more than 1,000,000 tons.
Solution – Task 4
Given:
• Reserves = 18,000,000 tons
• Constant extraction a = 300,000 tons/year (part a); 500,000 tons/year (part b)
• Growth scenario: a = 300,000 tons, k = 1.05, n = 10 years
Formula(s):
• Years to exhaustion at constant rate: t = Reserves / (annual extraction)
• Constant terms (k = 1 case): sn = a·n
• Finite geometric series (k ≠ 1): sn = a·(kn − 1)/(k − 1)
Step-by-Step Calculations:
t = 18,000,000 / 300,000 = 60 years.
t = 18,000,000 / 500,000 = 36 years.
Constant extraction of a each year for n years sums to a·n — exactly the k = 1
case, where every term in the series is identical.
1.0510 = 1.628894627.
s10 = 300,000·(1.628894627 − 1)/0.05 = 300,000 × 12.57789254 = 3,773,367.76 ≈
3,773,368 tons.
Constant-rate 10-year total = 300,000 × 10 = 3,000,000 tons.
Difference = 3,773,368 − 3,000,000 = 773,368 tons, which is less than 1,000,000
tons.
Explanation of Each Statement:

b) TRUE. Using the same reasoning with a faster extraction rate of 500,000 tons per year gives

Task 5. Coal Reserves and Declining Output
Difficulty: 2.1/5
A coal mining region has 9,000 million tons (9 billion tons) of estimated reserves. This year's output is 180
million tons, and analysts project output will fall by 3% every year forever, an infinite geometric series with a =
180 (million tons) and k = 0.97. A second scenario considers a steeper 5% annual decline (k = 0.95) starting
from the same output.
Statements (True / False):
a) Output in the second year is 174.6 million tons.
b) The infinite total extracted over all future years, under the 3% decline, is 6,000 million tons.
c) The reserves will never be fully exhausted, leaving 3 billion tons as permanently “stranded assets.”
d) If output instead declined 5% per year from the same 180 million-ton starting point, the total extracted
would leave less coal stranded than the 3%-decline case.
e) Under the 3% decline scenario, cumulative extraction after just the first 20 years alone would already
exceed the full infinite-horizon total of 6,000 million tons.
Solution – Task 5
Given:
• Reserves = 9,000 million tons
• a (this year's output) = 180 million tons
• k = 0.97 (3% annual decline); alternative k = 0.95 (5% annual decline)
Formula(s):
• Infinite sum (|k| < 1): a/(1 − k)
• Finite sum of first n terms: sn = a·(1 − kn)/(1 − k)
Step-by-Step Calculations:
Year-2 output = a·k = 180×0.97 = 174.6 million tons.
Infinite sum (3% decline) = 180/(1 − 0.97) = 180/0.03 = 6,000 million tons.
Stranded reserves (3% case) = 9,000 − 6,000 = 3,000 million tons (3 billion tons).
Infinite sum (5% decline) = 180/(1 − 0.95) = 180/0.05 = 3,600 million tons.
Stranded reserves (5% case) = 9,000 − 3,600 = 5,400 million tons — more than the
3,000 million tons stranded under the 3% case.
0.9720 ≈ 0.5438.
s20 = 180·(1 − 0.5438)/0.03 = 180 × 15.207 ≈ 2,737.3 million tons, which is far less
than 6,000 million tons.
Explanation of Each Statement:
a) TRUE. Year-2 output is the year-1 output multiplied once by the decline factor: 180×0.97 = 174.6 million tons,
matching exactly.

Task 6. Alternating Correction Payments in a Trading Model
Difficulty: 2.3/5
A quantitative trading model applies a diminishing corrective adjustment after each rebalancing: the first
adjustment is $4,000, and each subsequent adjustment reverses sign and is half the size of the previous one,
continuing indefinitely. This is an infinite geometric series with a = $4,000 and a negative quotient k = -0.5.
Statements (True / False):
a) The alternating adjustment series converges to a finite sum.
b) The infinite sum of all adjustments is approximately $2,666.67.
c) The sum of the first 4 adjustments is $3,000.00.
d) The series necessarily diverges regardless of its magnitude.
e) If the quotient were instead exactly k = -1, the partial sums would alternate forever between $4,000 and
$0.
Solution – Task 6
Given:
• a (first adjustment) = $4,000
• k (sign-reversing decay quotient) = -0.5
Formula(s):
• Infinite sum (|k| < 1): a/(1 − k)
• Special case k = -1: sn = a when n is odd, sn = 0 when n is even
Step-by-Step Calculations:
|k| = |-0.5| = 0.5 < 1, so the series converges.
Infinite sum = 4,000/(1 − (−0.5)) = 4,000/1.5 = $2,666.67.
First 4 terms: 4,000, then 4,000×(−0.5) = −2,000, then 4,000×0.25 = 1,000, then
4,000×(−0.125) = −500.
Sum of first 4 terms = 4,000 − 2,000 + 1,000 − 500 = $2,500.00 (not $3,000.00).
For k = -1: sn = a (odd n) or 0 (even n) — the partial sums oscillate forever and
never settle on a limit.
Explanation of Each Statement:
a) TRUE. Convergence depends only on the size of the ratio, |k|, not on whether that ratio is positive or negative.
Here k=−0.5, so |k|=0.5, which is below 1 — the alternating series still settles down to a fixed total even though its
terms flip sign at every step.
b) TRUE. The infinite-sum formula a/(1−k) applies the same way regardless of the sign of k, as long as |k|<1.
Substituting a=4,000 and k=−0.5 gives 4,000/(1−(−0.5)) = 4,000/1.5 ≈ $2,666.67.
c) FALSE. The first four adjustments are 4,000, then 4,000×(−0.5)=−2,000, then 4,000×0.25=1,000, then
4,000×(−0.125)=−500. Adding these gives 4,000−2,000+1,000−500 = $2,500.00, not $3,000.00.

d) FALSE. The sign of the ratio has no bearing on whether the series converges; only its magnitude matters. A
negative ratio simply means the terms alternate between adding to and subtracting from the running total, and as
long as |k| stays below 1, those swings shrink over time and the partial sums settle down — exactly what happens
here with |k|=0.5, so the series converges rather than diverging.
e) TRUE. With k=−1, each new term exactly cancels the one before it: starting from a, the running total becomes a,

Task 7. Equal vs. Growing Pension Contributions
Difficulty: 2.5/5
A city pension fund receives equal annual contributions of $12 million for 15 straight years (a degenerate
geometric series with k = 1). Fund managers also model an alternative in which contributions instead grow by
4% every year from the same $12 million starting point, a finite geometric series with a = 12 (million dollars), k =
1.04, and n = 15.
Statements (True / False):
a) This is the special k = 1 case of the geometric series, where the total simply equals the number of
payments multiplied by the payment amount.
b) The total contributions over the 15 years of equal $12 million payments equal $180.00 million.
c) Under the 4%-growth alternative, the 15-year total is approximately $240.11 million.
d) The 4%-growth 15-year total exceeds the no-growth total by more than $65.00 million.
e) Applying the general formula directly to the no-growth case would require dividing by zero.
Solution – Task 7
Given:
• Equal-payment case: a = $12 million/year, n = 15 years, k = 1
• Growth case: a = $12 million, k = 1.04, n = 15 years
Formula(s):
• k = 1 case: sn = a·n
• k ≠ 1 case: sn = a·(kn − 1)/(k − 1)
Step-by-Step Calculations:
Equal payments: s15 = 12 × 15 = $180.00 million.
1.0415 ≈ 1.800944.
s15 (growth case) = 12·(1.800944 − 1)/0.04 = 12 × 20.0236 = $240.28 million.
Difference = 240.28 − 180.00 = $60.28 million, which is not more than $65.00
million.
The general formula's denominator is (k − 1); at k = 1 this denominator is 0, so
the formula is undefined and the k = 1 case must instead use sn = a·n.
Explanation of Each Statement:
a) TRUE. When every payment is identical, there is no changing ratio between consecutive terms — or
equivalently, the ratio is exactly 1. Adding n copies of the same number a together is simply n×a, a much simpler
formula than the ratio-based one needed when the terms actually change size from year to year, and it sidesteps
the division-by-zero problem that the general formula would run into at k=1.
b) TRUE. With 15 identical annual payments of $12 million each, the total is just 12×15 = $180.00 million; no
compounding or ratio calculation is needed since nothing changes from one year to the next.

d) FALSE. The actual gap between the 4%-growth total (≈$240.28 million) and the flat total ($180.00 million) is
