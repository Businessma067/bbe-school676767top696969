  A (true): The present value needed to fund the 20-year annuity is approximately \$57,349.67.
  B (true): The perpetuity value is approximately \$83,333.33, exceeding the 20-year annuity's present value by approximately \$25,983.66.

  D (false): If the term were extended to 40 years, the present value would rise to MORE than 95% of the perpetuity value.
  E (true): The perpetuity formula is obtained by letting the number of payments grow toward infinity in the annuity present-value formula, so that the value converges to \$83,333.33 as the limiting value.
OVERVIEW: A nonprofit organization needs to fund \$5,000 scholarship payments at the end of each year for 20 years, with an interest rate of 6%. The board also wants to compare this cost to funding the same \$5,000 annual payment in perpetuity at the same rate.

Annual payment a = \$5,000

Nominal annual rate r = 6% = 0.06

Compounding: annual, ordinary annuity / perpetuity

n = 20 years (and 40 years for part d)

**Part 2: Formula.**

$P_n=(a/r)[1-1/(1+r)^{n}]$

Perpetuity: $P=a/r$ (limit as $n\to\infty$)

**Part 3: Solve.**

**1.** $P_{20} = (5,000/0.06)[1-1/(1.06)^{20}] = 83,333.33 \times 0.688195 = \$57,349.67$.

**5.** As $n$ grows without bound, $(1+r)^{n}\to\infty$, so $1/(1+r)^{n}\to0$ and $P_n\to a/r = 83,333.33$, confirming the perpetuity limit.
