TASK 1 — Balancing Crate Counts Between Two Depots
Format: Internal Memo  |  Difficulty: 1.0/10  |  (previously Task 1 (orig. Tasks 1–10 set))
The North depot and the South depot are together holding 620 crates this week. A scheduling note observes that if 50 crates were
transferred from North to South, the two depots would end up holding exactly the same number of crates.
GIVEN
Let x = crates at the North depot, y = crates at the South depot. The memo's transfer clue must be translated into an equation: after
moving 50 crates from North to South, North would have x − 50 and South would have y + 50, and these are described as equal.
STATEMENTS
A. The North depot currently holds 360 crates.
B. The South depot currently holds 240 crates.
C. If 30 crates were moved from South to North instead, North would then hold 390 crates.
D. The difference between the two depots today is 120 crates.
E. Moving 50 crates from North to South (as the memo describes) would leave both depots holding 310 crates each.
MATHEMATICAL MODEL
x + y = 620 (combined total)
x − 50 = y + 50, which rearranges to x − y = 100
STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION
This is now a standard sum-and-difference pair: x + y = 620 and x − y = 100. Adding the two equations eliminates y: 2x = 720, so x = 360.
Then y = 620 − 360 = 260. Check: 360 − 50 = 310 and 260 + 50 = 310 — the two depots do end up equal, confirming the translation was
correct.
Final Answer: North depot = 360 crates  |  South depot = 260 crates
STATEMENT ANALYSIS
#
Verdict
Explanation
A
TRUE
The elimination step gives x = 360, and this also satisfies the memo's own equalizing check, so it is consistent both algebraically and in
context.
B
FALSE
The South depot holds 260 crates, not 240. A common slip is assuming the difference (100) can simply be split from the total the same way
as North's value, instead of substituting back into x + y = 620.
C
TRUE
Moving crates the other way adds to North's total rather than subtracting: 360 + 30 = 390.
D
FALSE
The actual difference today is 360 − 260 = 100 crates. This trap comes from misreading the memo's '50 crates transferred' as itself being
the current difference, when in fact a 50-crate transfer is what makes the totals equal, not what separates them today.
E
TRUE
This is exactly the memo's own claim, confirmed by the numbers: 360 − 50 = 310 and 260 + 50 = 310.
GENERAL EXPLANATION
The whole task hinges on correctly translating one sentence ("transferring 50 crates would equalize the depots") into the algebraic statement x −
50 = y + 50, rather than mistaking the transfer amount for the current difference between the depots. Once that translation is made, this is the
simplest possible sum-and-difference system: no fees, no percentages, no unit conversions, and both unknowns come out as round numbers. The
main traps are statement B (confusing total-minus-difference logic) and statement D (conflating the equalizing transfer with the current gap).

Silverline Stationery Co. received two invoices from the same supplier this month. Notebooks and pens are billed at fixed unit prices that
stayed the same on both invoices.
Invoice
Notebooks
Pens
Invoice Total
#101
40
25
$185.00
#102
15
60
$160.50
GIVEN
Let x = price of one notebook, y = price of one pen.
STATEMENTS
A. A notebook costs $3.50.
B. A pen costs $2.10.
C. Invoice #101 (40 notebooks and 25 pens) totals $185.00.
D. 10 notebooks and 10 pens purchased together would cost $53.00.
E. Invoice #102 (15 notebooks and 60 pens) totals $172.50.
MATHEMATICAL MODEL
40x + 25y = 185.00 (Invoice #101)
15x + 60y = 160.50 (Invoice #102)
STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION
Divide Invoice #102 by 15 to simplify: x + 4y = 10.70, so x = 10.70 − 4y. Substitute into Invoice #101: 40(10.70 − 4y) + 25y = 185.00.
This expands to 428.00 − 160y + 25y = 185.00, so −135y = −243.00, giving y = 1.80. Then x = 10.70 − 4(1.80) = 3.50.
Final Answer: Notebook = $3.50  |  Pen = $1.80
STATEMENT ANALYSIS
#
Verdict
Explanation
A
TRUE
The unit price recovered for a notebook is $3.50, and it satisfies both invoices simultaneously, not just one of them.
B
FALSE
A pen actually costs $1.80, not $2.10. Rounding −135y = −243.00 carelessly is the most likely source of this kind of error.
C
TRUE
This is simply the invoice total as printed, exactly reproduced by 40(3.50) + 25(1.80).
D
TRUE
10(3.50) + 10(1.80) = 35.00 + 18.00 = $53.00; note this is NOT the same as averaging either invoice total, since the notebook-to-pen ratio
here differs from both invoices.
E
FALSE
Invoice #102's printed total is $160.50, not $172.50 — a student who mixes up which invoice number goes with which total would produce
this kind of error.
GENERAL EXPLANATION
A clean, single-method elimination-by-substitution task with no reconstruction or claim-checking layer — both invoices state their totals directly.
The only genuine trap is decimal carelessness during the substitution step (statement B) and simple misreading of which printed total belongs to
which invoice (statement E); statement D checks whether the reader understands that a new quantity combination must be recalculated rather
than interpolated from the two given invoices.

The Riverside Community Cinema's box-office system logged ticket counts and revenue for two Saturday screenings of the same film.
Adult and child tickets are sold at fixed prices throughout the day.
Session
Adult Tickets
Child Tickets
Revenue
Saturday Matinee
90
150
$2,130
Saturday Evening
160
40
$2,200
GIVEN
Let a = adult ticket price, c = child ticket price.
STATEMENTS
A. An adult ticket costs $12.00.
B. A child ticket costs $7.00.
C. The Saturday matinee (90 adult, 150 child) generated $2,050.00 in revenue.
D. The Saturday evening session (160 adult, 40 child) generated $2,300.00 in revenue.
E. 50 adult tickets and 50 child tickets together would generate $1,000.00.
MATHEMATICAL MODEL
90a + 150c = 2130 (Saturday matinee)
160a + 40c = 2200 (Saturday evening)
STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION
Divide the evening equation by 40: 4a + c = 55, so c = 55 − 4a. Substitute into the matinee equation: 90a + 150(55 − 4a) = 2130. This
expands to 90a + 8250 − 600a = 2130, so −510a = −6120, giving a = 12. Then c = 55 − 48 = 7.
Final Answer: Adult ticket = $12.00  |  Child ticket = $7.00
STATEMENT ANALYSIS
#
Verdict
Explanation
A
TRUE
The recovered adult price of $12.00 reproduces both session totals correctly.
B
TRUE
The recovered child price of $7.00 likewise reproduces both totals; swapping which price belongs to 'adult' and which to 'child' is a common
mix-up when the two totals are close in size.
C
FALSE
Using the actual prices, the matinee total is 90(12) + 150(7) = $2,130.00, not $2,050.00 — this simply misquotes the figure already printed
in the summary table.
D
FALSE
The evening total works out to 160(12) + 40(7) = $2,200.00, not $2,300.00 — a $100 overstatement of the value already given.
E
FALSE
50(12) + 50(7) = 600 + 350 = $950.00, not $1,000.00. This combination appears in neither session, so it must be computed fresh rather
than estimated from the table.
GENERAL EXPLANATION
A textbook two-total elimination problem. The main thing being tested is not the algebra itself but attentiveness: statements C and D simply
misquote figures that are already printed in the table (testing whether the reader re-derives values instead of trusting a plausible-sounding but
wrong number), while statement E requires computing a genuinely new quantity combination rather than guessing at it.

TASK 4 — Stripping Out a Delivery Fee Before Pricing a Deli's Menu
Format: Delivery Receipts  |  Difficulty: 1.5/10  |  (previously Task 4 (orig. Tasks 1–10 set))
Corner Deli delivers office lunches for a flat $8.00 delivery fee added on top of the food cost, no matter the order size. Receipt A: 6
sandwiches, 4 wraps, plus the $8.00 delivery fee — total charged $70.00. Receipt B: 3 sandwiches, 9 wraps, plus the $8.00 delivery fee —
total charged $74.00.
GIVEN
Let x = price of one sandwich, y = price of one wrap. Before writing any equation, the $8.00 delivery fee must be subtracted from each
receipt total, since it is not part of either unknown price.
STATEMENTS
A. A sandwich costs $7.00.
B. A wrap costs $5.00.
C. Receipt A's food subtotal, before the $8.00 delivery fee is added, is $62.00.
D. Receipt B's total, including the $8.00 delivery fee, is $74.00.
E. A pickup order (no delivery fee) of 5 sandwiches and 5 wraps would cost $60.00.
MATHEMATICAL MODEL
6x + 4y = 70.00 − 8.00 = 62.00
3x + 9y = 74.00 − 8.00 = 66.00
STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION
Multiply the second equation by 2: 6x + 18y = 132. Subtract the first equation: 14y = 70, so y = 5. Substitute back: 3x + 9(5) = 66, so 3x
= 21, and x = 7.
Final Answer: Sandwich = $7.00  |  Wrap = $5.00
STATEMENT ANALYSIS
#
Verdict
Explanation
A
TRUE
Once the $8.00 fee is removed from both receipts, the food-only system correctly gives $7.00 per sandwich; using the receipt totals (70 and
74) directly would instead produce the wrong prices.
B
TRUE
The wrap price of $5.00 likewise depends on subtracting the fee first — this is the main interpretive step in the task, not the algebra itself.
C
TRUE
70.00 − 8.00 = $62.00, matching Receipt A's food-only subtotal used to build the equation.
D
TRUE
Receipt B's printed total, delivery fee included, is $74.00 — note this includes the fee, unlike the $66.00 figure used inside the equation
itself.
E
TRUE
A pickup order has no delivery fee to add: 5(7) + 5(5) = $60.00.
GENERAL EXPLANATION
The entire difficulty of this task is front-loaded into one interpretive step: recognizing that the flat $8.00 fee must be subtracted from both totals
before the system is built, since it is a per-order charge, not a per-item price. Once that is done, the elimination itself is trivial. Every statement
here is TRUE, which is itself worth noting — a reader who skips the fee-removal step would get the wrong x and y and likely start marking
correct statements FALSE by mistake.

TASK 5 — Splitting Savings Between Two Interest-Bearing Accounts
Format: Personal Finance Summary  |  Difficulty: 1.6/10  |  (previously Task 5 (orig. Tasks 1–10 set))
An investor split a total of $10,000 between two accounts at the start of the year: Account A pays 4% simple annual interest, and Account
B pays 7% simple annual interest. No deposits or withdrawals were made all year, and together the two accounts earned $520.00 in
interest.
GIVEN
Let x = amount placed in Account A, y = amount placed in Account B.
STATEMENTS
A. $6,500 was placed in Account A.
B. $4,500 was placed in Account B.
C. Account A earned $260.00 in interest over the year.
D. Account B earned $210.00 in interest over the year.
E. Had the entire $10,000 been placed in Account B alone, total interest for the year would have been $700.00.
MATHEMATICAL MODEL
x + y = 10000 (total split)
0.04x + 0.07y = 520 (total interest)
STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION
Solve the first equation for x: x = 10000 − y. Substitute: 0.04(10000 − y) + 0.07y = 520. This expands to 400 − 0.04y + 0.07y = 520, so
0.03y = 120, and y = 4000. Then x = 10000 − 4000 = 6000.
Final Answer: Account A = $6,000  |  Account B = $4,000
STATEMENT ANALYSIS
#
Verdict
Explanation
A
FALSE
Account A actually holds $6,000, not $6,500. Because the two rates (4% and 7%) are close together, it is easy to mis-split the $10,000
roughly in half instead of solving the weighted equation properly.
B
FALSE
Account B holds $4,000, not $4,500 — the same rough 50/50 guess produces this error too, and the two wrong figures would not even add
to $10,000, a useful way to catch the mistake.
C
FALSE
Account A's interest is 0.04 × 6,000 = $240.00, not $260.00. This kind of error often comes from applying a slightly wrong rate or a small
arithmetic slip.
D
FALSE
Account B's interest is 0.07 × 4,000 = $280.00, not $210.00. Note $240.00 + $280.00 = $520.00 matches the given total, confirming these
(not statements C/D) are correct.
E
TRUE
Placing the full $10,000 at the 7% rate gives 0.07 × 10,000 = $700.00, which is indeed higher than the $520.00 actually earned from the
mixed allocation.
GENERAL EXPLANATION
This is the first task in the set to introduce percentage-rate coefficients (0.04 and 0.07) rather than simple per-unit prices, which is a small but
real jump in abstraction. Every one of statements A–D is deliberately wrong, all clustered around the same natural mistake: eyeballing a rough
50/50 split instead of weighting by the two different rates. Statement E is the only true one, and it rewards actually multiplying through rather
than assuming a blended-rate shortcut.

TASK 6 — Pricing Two Chair Grades From a Price Gap and a Shipment Value
Format: Stock Valuation Report  |  Difficulty: 1.8/10  |  (previously Task 6 (orig. Tasks 1–10 set))
Premium-grade office chairs are priced exactly $45 more per unit than Standard-grade chairs throughout the current catalogue. A recent
shipment of 18 Standard chairs and 12 Premium chairs was valued at $9,660.00 in total.
GIVEN
Let x = price of a Standard chair, y = price of a Premium chair.
STATEMENTS
A. A Standard chair is priced at $304.00.
B. A Premium chair is priced at $354.00.
C. The 12 Premium chairs in the shipment are worth $4,188.00 in total.
D. The price gap between one Premium chair and one Standard chair is $45.00.
E. A smaller order of 5 Standard chairs and 5 Premium chairs would cost more than $3,000.00.
MATHEMATICAL MODEL
y = x + 45 (Premium priced $45 above Standard)
18x + 12y = 9660 (shipment value)
STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION
Substitute directly: 18x + 12(x + 45) = 9660. This expands to 18x + 12x + 540 = 9660, so 30x = 9120, and x = 304. Then y = 304 + 45 =
349.
Final Answer: Standard chair = $304.00  |  Premium chair = $349.00
STATEMENT ANALYSIS
#
Verdict
Explanation
A
TRUE
The Standard chair price of $304.00 is what makes the shipment value work out exactly, once the $45 price gap is built into the second
equation.
B
FALSE
A Premium chair costs $349.00, not $354.00 — a tempting but incorrect shortcut is rounding $304 up to $310 first and then adding $45.
C
TRUE
12 × 349.00 = $4,188.00, part of the shipment total of $9,660.00 (the remaining $5,472.00 comes from the 18 Standard chairs).
D
TRUE
This is simply the relationship stated in the report and used to build the second equation: 349 − 304 = $45.00.
E
TRUE
5(304) + 5(349) = 1,520 + 1,745 = $3,265.00, which is indeed more than $3,000.00 — this smaller order uses a different ratio (5-and-5)
than the shipment (18-and-12), so it must be recalculated rather than scaled down.
GENERAL EXPLANATION
A relation-plus-total system solved by direct substitution rather than elimination, since one variable is already isolated. The only trap is statement
B's rounding shortcut; everything else rewards careful direct computation from the two solved prices.

TASK 7 — Extracting a Hidden Rate Structure From a Mobile Ad
Format: Advertisement  |  Difficulty: 1.9/10  |  (previously Task 7 (orig. Tasks 1–10 set))
ByteMobile's ad boasts a "simple plan": one customer who went 40 minutes over their allowance last month paid $29.00 in total. A heavy
user who went 120 minutes over paid $53.00 — the ad frames this as a low, predictable rate for every extra minute, on top of a small fixed
monthly fee.
GIVEN
Let f = fixed monthly fee, r = rate charged per extra minute. The advertisement never states the fixed fee or the rate directly — both must
be recovered from the two example bills it quotes.
STATEMENTS
A. ByteMobile's fixed monthly fee is $17.00.
B. The extra-minute rate advertised is $0.30 per minute.
C. A customer using 200 extra minutes in a month would pay $80.00.
D. A customer using 0 extra minutes would pay $0.00 that month.
E. The advertised rate ($0.30 per minute) is more than double a rival plan's rate of $0.20 per minute.
MATHEMATICAL MODEL
f + 40r = 29.00
f + 120r = 53.00
STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION
Subtract the first equation from the second: 80r = 24.00, so r = 0.30. Substitute back: f + 12.00 = 29.00, so f = 17.00.
Final Answer: Fixed fee = $17.00  |  Extra-minute rate = $0.30/min
STATEMENT ANALYSIS
#
Verdict
Explanation
A
TRUE
The $17.00 fixed fee is what remains once the per-minute charge is subtracted out of either quoted bill.
B
TRUE
The $0.30 rate is recovered from the DIFFERENCE between the two bills (a $24.00 gap over 80 extra minutes), not from either bill in
isolation.
C
FALSE
The actual charge is 17.00 + 200(0.30) = $77.00, not $80.00.
D
FALSE
Even with zero extra minutes, the fixed fee of $17.00 still applies, so the bill would be $17.00, not $0.00 — the ad describes extra-minute
charges on top of a fee, not instead of one.
E
FALSE
Double the rival's $0.20 rate would be $0.40, and ByteMobile's $0.30 rate is lower than that, not more than double it.
GENERAL EXPLANATION
A clean subtract-directly elimination (both equations share the same fixed-fee coefficient), the same mechanical shape used repeatedly at higher
tiers of this worksheet for phone/utility/taxi-style problems. The traps are conceptual rather than computational: statement D tests whether the
reader understands that a fixed fee still applies at zero usage, and statement E tests careless multiplication when comparing to a competitor's
rate.

Format: Production Report  |  Difficulty: 2.1/10  |  (previously Task 8 (orig. Tasks 1–10 set))
The table below lists standard specifications for two oven models. This week, the division completed 130 ovens in total and logged 795
assembly hours in total across both models. (Not every figure below is needed to find how many of each model were built.)
Product
Assembly Hrs/Unit
Material Cost/Unit
Standard Oven
4 hrs
$120
Deluxe Oven
9 hrs
$180
GIVEN
Let s = number of Standard ovens built, d = number of Deluxe ovens built. The material-cost column is not needed to find s and d — it
becomes relevant only for statement E below.
STATEMENTS
A. The division built 75 Standard ovens this week.
B. The division built 45 Deluxe ovens this week.
C. Standard ovens accounted for 300 assembly hours this week.
D. Deluxe ovens accounted for 500 assembly hours this week.
E. The total material cost of all Standard ovens built this week is $9,000.00.
MATHEMATICAL MODEL
s + d = 130 (total ovens built)
4s + 9d = 795 (total assembly hours)
STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION
From the total-ovens equation, s = 130 − d. Substitute into the hours equation: 4(130 − d) + 9d = 795. This expands to 520 − 4d + 9d =
795, giving 5d = 275, so d = 55. Then s = 130 − 55 = 75.
Final Answer: Standard ovens = 75  |  Deluxe ovens = 55
STATEMENT ANALYSIS
#
Verdict
Explanation
A
TRUE
The unit and hours totals together pin down 75 Standard ovens; the material-cost column plays no role in finding this figure.
B
FALSE
The division built 55 Deluxe ovens, not 45 — a common error is subtracting the hours figures instead of properly eliminating a variable,
which tends to understate d.
C
TRUE
4 hours × 75 Standard ovens = 300 hours, part of the 795-hour weekly total (the remaining 495 hours come from the Deluxe ovens).
D
FALSE
Deluxe ovens actually used 9 × 55 = 495 hours, not 500 — note 300 + 495 = 795 matches the report's total exactly, confirming 495 is
correct.
E
TRUE
This is where the material-cost column finally matters: 75 Standard ovens × $120 each = $9,000.00. This statement could not have been
answered from the hours data alone.
GENERAL EXPLANATION
A count-and-rate system (units + hours) rather than a price system, with one deliberately irrelevant column (material cost) planted to test
selective reading — it turns out to matter only for the very last statement, which flips the "irrelevant data" expectation on its head.

Two branches sold sofas and armchairs this month at company-wide fixed prices. "Net sales" (gross sales minus returns) is what actually
reflects items sold at their listed prices.
Branch
Sofas Sold
Armchairs Sold
Gross Sales
Returns
Riverside
14
22
$9,760
$460
Hillcrest
20
10
$9,300
$300
GIVEN
Let x = price of one sofa, y = price of one armchair. The gross figures cannot be used directly — each branch's returns must be subtracted
first to isolate the value of items actually sold at listed prices.
STATEMENTS
A. A sofa sells for $350.00.
B. An armchair sells for $200.00.
C. Riverside's net sales (after its $460 in returns) were $9,300.00.
D. Hillcrest's gross sales (before its $300 in returns) were $9,300.00.
E. Had Riverside recorded zero returns that month, its gross and net sales would both have equalled $9,760.00.

STATEMENT ANALYSIS
#
Verdict
Explanation
A
TRUE
The $350.00 sofa price only emerges correctly once each branch's returns have been subtracted from gross sales; using the gross figures
directly would give a different (incorrect) system.
B
TRUE
The armchair price of $200.00 depends on the same return-deduction step as the sofa price.

GENERAL EXPLANATION
A genuinely elegant "add and subtract" system: adding the two allocation equations directly gives x + y, and subtracting gives y − x, sidestepping
a traditional elimination-by-multiplication entirely. Statement C tests whether the reader mistakenly assumes the sum of two partial-allocation
returns should be comparable to a single full-allocation return, when in fact they describe entirely different amounts of money earning interest.

TASK 59 — Cedar Hollow Reserve — Linear Population Growth Reconstruction
Format: Field Biology Report  |  Difficulty: 9.8/10  |  (previously Task 59 (orig. Tasks 41–60 set))
Two species change by a fixed net number of individuals each year. At Year 2: Species A = 610, Species B = 730 (combined 1,340). At
Year 6: combined population = 1,772. Species A grows at exactly twice the annual rate of Species B.
GIVEN
Let x = Species A's net annual change (individuals/year), y = Species B's net annual change.
STATEMENTS
A. By Year 6, Species A's population exceeds Species B's population by more than 20 individuals.
B. If Species B's growth rate were instead equal to Species A's actual rate, the combined population at Year 6 would exceed the actual
combined 1,772 by more than 140 individuals.
C. The ratio of the two species' total population growth from Year 2 to Year 6 (Species A's growth : Species B's growth) is greater than 2.5
: 1.
D. At some point between Year 2 and Year 6, the two species had equal populations.
E. Species A overtakes Species B in total population size before Year 5.
MATHEMATICAL MODEL
x + y = 108 (combined growth of 432 over 4 years, Year 2 to Year 6: 4x+4y=432)
x = 2y (Species A grows at twice Species B's rate)
STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION
Substitute the second into the first: 2y + y = 108, so y = 36. Then x = 2(36) = 72.
Final Answer: Species A = +72 individuals/year  |  Species B = +36 individuals/year
STATEMENT ANALYSIS
#
Verdict
Explanation
A
TRUE
At Year 6: Species A = 610 + 4(72) = 898; Species B = 730 + 4(36) = 874. The gap is 24, which exceeds 20.
B
TRUE
If Species B also grew at 72/year: Species B at Year 6 = 730 + 4(72) = 1,018. New combined total = 898 + 1,018 = 1,916, exceeding the
actual 1,772 by 144 — more than 140.
C
FALSE
Species A's growth over 4 years is 4(72) = 288; Species B's is 4(36) = 144. The ratio is 288/144 = 2.0, not greater than 2.5.
D
TRUE
At Year 2, Species B leads (730 vs. 610). At Year 6, Species A leads (898 vs. 874). Since both change at a constant linear rate, this reversal
means they must have been exactly equal at some point in between.
E
FALSE
Setting 610 + 72(t−2) = 730 + 36(t−2) gives 36(t−2) = 120, so t−2 ≈ 3.33, t ≈ 5.33 — the crossover happens after Year 5, not before.
GENERAL EXPLANATION
A linear-growth word problem where the two equations come from a combined-growth total and a stated ratio between the two growth rates,
rather than from two separate direct observations. Statement D tests an intermediate-value-style argument (a lead reversal implies a crossing
point) without requiring the exact crossing time, while statement E then demands that exact time and checks it against a specific year.
