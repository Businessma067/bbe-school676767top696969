a) Rider P qualifies for the discount.
b) Rider Q, who is 67, has a qualifying disability, but earns $50,000, does NOT qualify for the discount because 
the income exceeds $20,000.
c) Having an income above $20,000 automatically disqualifies a senior rider from the discount, even if they 
have a qualifying disability.
d) There exists a scenario where a 70-year-old rider with no disability and an income of $25,000 would NOT 
qualify for the discount.
e) Age alone (being at least 65) is a sufficient condition for receiving the discount, regardless of disability or 
income status.
Explanations:
a) True. P is 70 (≥65) and earns $18,000 (< $20,000), satisfying the income part of the OR-condition, so both 
the age requirement and the OR-condition are met.
b) False. Trap: Q is 67 (≥65) and has a qualifying disability, which alone satisfies the OR-condition regardless 
of income. Q's high income is irrelevant here — the disability is enough, so Q DOES qualify.
c) False. Q's case directly disproves this — Q has a high income ($50,000) but still qualifies because the 
disability alone satisfies the OR-condition.
d) True. Such a rider meets the age requirement but satisfies neither part of the OR-condition (no disability, and 
$25,000 is not below $20,000), so the overall AND-condition fails and they would not qualify.
e) False. As shown in (d), meeting the age requirement alone is not enough — the OR-condition (disability or 
low income) must also be satisfied.
