This statement asserts that the probability of a flush is more than $8$ times the probability of four of a kind. To verify this, we can compare the ratio of the number of ways to achieve these hands, as the total number of hands ($N_{\text{total}}$) will cancel out in the probability ratio.

The number of ways to form a flush, $N_{\text{flush}}$, is found in the solution overview:

$$
N_{\text{four of a kind}} = \binom{13}{1} \binom{4}{4} \times \binom{48}{1}
$$

$$
\binom{13}{1} = \dfrac{13!}{1!(13-1)!}
$$

$$
\binom{13}{1} = 13
$$

$$
\binom{4}{4} = 1
$$

$$
\binom{48}{1} = \dfrac{48!}{1!(48-1)!}
$$

$$
\binom{48}{1} = 48
$$

$$
13 \times 1 = 13
$$

$$
13 \times 48 = 624
$$

$$
N_{\text{flush}} = 5,148
$$

The number of ways to form four of a kind, $N_{\text{four of a kind}}$, is also found in the solution overview:

$$
N_{\text{four of a kind}} = 624
$$

Now, we compute the ratio of these counts:

$$
\frac{N_{\text{flush}}}{N_{\text{four of a kind}}} = \frac{5,148}{624}
$$

$$
\frac{N_{\text{flush}}}{N_{\text{four of a kind}}} \approx 8.2499
$$

The ratio indicates that a flush is approximately $8.25$ times more likely than four of a kind. Since $8.2499 > 8$, the probability of a flush is indeed more than $8$ times the probability of four of a kind.
