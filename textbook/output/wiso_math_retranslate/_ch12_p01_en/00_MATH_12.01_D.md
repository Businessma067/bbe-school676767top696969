This statement asks for the probability that the committee contains at least one woman. It is often easier to calculate the probability of the complementary event: that the committee contains no women (i.e., it consists of all men), and then subtract this from $1$. We have already calculated the probability of an all-men committee in statement C.

$$
P(\text{at least one woman}) = 1 - P(\text{all men})
$$

Using the probability calculated in statement C:

$$
P(\text{at least one woman}) = 1 - \frac{35}{495}

$$
P(\text{at least one woman}) = \frac{495 - 35}{495}
$$

$$
P(\text{at least one woman}) = \frac{460}{495}
$$

$$
P(\text{at least one woman}) \approx 0.929293
$$
