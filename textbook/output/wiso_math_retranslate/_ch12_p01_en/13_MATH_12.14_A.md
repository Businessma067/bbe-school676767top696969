This statement asks for the total number of distinct $5$-person committees that can be formed from the $13$ club members. Since the order of selection does not matter, this is a combination problem. The number of combinations of choosing $k$ items from a set of $N$ is given by $\binom{N}{k}$.

Here, $N = 13$ total members and $k = 5$ committee members.

$$
\text{Number of committees} = \binom{13}{5}
$$

$$
\binom{13}{5} = \dfrac{13!}{5!(13-5)!}
$$

$$
\dfrac{13!}{5!(8)!} = \dfrac{13 \cdot 12 \cdot 11 \cdot 10 \cdot 9}{1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 4 \cdot 5}
$$

$$
13 \cdot 12 = 156
$$

$$
156 \cdot 11 = 1716
$$

$$
1716 \cdot 10 = 17160
$$

$$
17160 \cdot 9 = 154440
$$

$$
\dfrac{154440}{120} = 1287
$$

$$
\binom{13}{5} = 1287
$$

There are $1287$ possible $5$-person committees. The claim states that the total number of possible $5$-person committees is $1287$.

So the statement is True.
