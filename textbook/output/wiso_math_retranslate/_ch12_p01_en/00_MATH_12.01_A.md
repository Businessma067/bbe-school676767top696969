This statement asks for the total number of possible committees. As determined in the solution overview, the total number of ways to select a committee of $4$ people from $12$ members is given by the combination formula $\binom{12}{4}$.

$$
N(\text{total committees}) = \binom{12}{4}
$$

$$
\binom{12}{4} = \dfrac{12!}{4!(12-4)!}
$$

$$
\dfrac{12!}{4!(8)!} = \dfrac{12 \cdot 11 \cdot 10 \cdot 9}{1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 4}
$$

$$
12 \cdot 11 = 132
$$

$$
132 \cdot 10 = 1320
$$

$$
1320 \cdot 9 = 11880
$$

$$
\dfrac{11880}{24} = 495
$$

$$
\binom{12}{4} = 495
$$

So the statement is False.
