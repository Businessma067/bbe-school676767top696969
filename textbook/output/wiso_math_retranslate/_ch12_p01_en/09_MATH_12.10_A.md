The claim is about the probability of rolling at least one 6 in 4 rolls of a single die. Let $A$ be this event. The most straightforward way to calculate this is using the complement rule: $P(A) = 1 - P(\text{no 6 in 4 rolls})$. The probability of not rolling a 6 on a single roll is $\frac{5}{6}$. Since the 4 rolls are independent, the probability of not rolling a 6 in any of the 4 rolls is the product of the individual probabilities.

$$
P(A) = 1 - \left(\frac{5}{6}\right)^4
$$

$$
P(A) = 1 - \frac{625}{1296}
$$

$$
P(A) \approx 1 - 0.482253
$$

$$
P(A) \approx 0.517747
$$

When expressed as a percentage, this is approximately $51.77\%$. The claim states that this probability is greater than $50\%$.

So the statement is True.
