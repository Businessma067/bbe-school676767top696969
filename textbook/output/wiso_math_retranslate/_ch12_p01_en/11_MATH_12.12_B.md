This claim asks for the number of possible codes if they were 4 digits long, still without repeats and with order mattering, using the same set of $n$ available digits. This is a permutation problem, $P(n, k)$, where $k=4$.

$$
P(n, k) = \frac{n!}{(n-k)!}
$$

Substituting $n=8$ and $k=4$:

$$
P(8, 4) = \frac{8!}{(8-4)!}
$$

$$
P(8, 4) = \frac{8!}{4!}
$$

$$
P(8, 4) = 8 \times 7 \times 6 \times 5

$$
8 \times 7 = 56
$$

$$
56 \times 6 = 336

336 \times 5 = 1680
$$

$$
P(8, 4) = 1,680
