This statement asks for the probability that a specific child receives no candy at all. Let's designate one child as 'Child 1'. If Child 1 receives $0$ candies, the remaining $10$ candies must be distributed among the other $4-1=3$ children. This is equivalent to finding the number of non-negative integer solutions to $x_2 + x_3 + x_4 = 10$.

Using the stars-and-bars formula with $n=10$ items and $k=3$ children:

$$
N_{\text{Child 1 receives } 0} = \binom{10+3-1}{3-1}
$$

$$
N_{\text{Child 1 receives } 0} = \binom{12}{2}
$$

$$
\binom{12}{2} = \dfrac{12!}{2!(12-2)!}
$$

$$
\dfrac{12!}{2!(10)!} = \dfrac{12 \cdot 11}{1 \cdot 2}
$$

$$
12 \cdot 11 = 132
$$

$$
\dfrac{132}{2} = 66
$$

$$
\binom{12}{2} = 66
$$

$$
N_{\text{Child 1 receives } 0} = \frac{12 \times 11}{2 \times 1}
$$

$$
N_{\text{Child 1 receives } 0} = 66
$$

The probability of this event is the ratio of the number of favorable outcomes to the total number of outcomes, $N_{\text{total}} = 286$, as calculated in the overview.

$$
P(\text{specific child receives } 0) = \frac{66}{286}
$$

$$
P(\text{specific child receives } 0) \approx 0.230769
$$

As a percentage, this is approximately $23.08\%$. The claim states this probability is greater than $23\%$.

So the statement is True.
