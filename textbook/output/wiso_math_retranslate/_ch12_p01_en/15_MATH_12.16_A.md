The claim asks for the probability that no two of the 8 people share a birthday. This means that all 8 people must have distinct birthdays. We calculate this probability by finding the number of ways 8 people can have distinct birthdays and dividing it by the total number of possible birthday arrangements for 8 people.

The general formula for the probability that $N$ people have distinct birthdays (from $D$ possible days) is:

$$
P(\text{no shared birthday}) = \frac{P(D, N)}{D^N} = \frac{D \times (D-1) \times \dots \times (D-N+1)}{D^N}
$$

For $N=8$ people and $D=365$ days, the calculation is:

$$
P(\text{no shared birthday}) = \frac{365 \times 364 \times 363 \times 362 \times 361 \times 360 \times 359 \times 358}{365^8}
$$

$$
P(\text{no shared birthday}) \approx 0.925665
$$

Converting this to a percentage gives $92.5665\%$. The claim states this probability is greater than $92.5\%$.

So the statement is True.
