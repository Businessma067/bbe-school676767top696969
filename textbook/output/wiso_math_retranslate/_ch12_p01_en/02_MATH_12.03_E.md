This claim compares the probability of all 3 girls sitting together with the probability of an alternating arrangement. Specifically, it asks if the former is more than 5 times the latter.

From statement C, the probability that all 3 girls sit together is $P(\text{girls together}) = \frac{1}{7}$.
From statement D, the probability of an alternating arrangement is $P(\text{alternating}) = \frac{1}{35}$.

To compare these, we can find their ratio:

$$
\frac{P(\text{girls together})}{P(\text{alternating})} = \dfrac{1}{7}\cdot\dfrac{35}{1} = 5
$$

This means the probability that all 3 girls sit together is exactly 5 times the probability of an alternating arrangement. The claim states that it is *more than* 5 times.

So the statement is False.
