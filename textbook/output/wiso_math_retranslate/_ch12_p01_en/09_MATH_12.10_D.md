The claim is about the probability of rolling at least one double-six in 24 rolls of a pair of dice. Let $B$ be this event. We calculate this probability using the complement rule: $P(B) = 1 - P(\text{no double-six in 24 rolls})$. The probability of rolling a double-six on a single throw of a pair of dice is $\frac{1}{36}$. Therefore, the probability of not rolling a double-six on a single throw is $1 - \frac{1}{36} = \frac{35}{36}$. Since the 24 rolls are independent, we raise this probability to the power of 24.

$$
P(B) = 1 - \left(\frac{35}{36}\right)^{24}
$$

$$
P(B) \approx 1 - 0.508596
$$

$$
P(B) \approx 0.491404
$$

When expressed as a percentage, this is approximately $49.14\%$. The claim states that this probability is less than $50\%$.

So the statement is True.
