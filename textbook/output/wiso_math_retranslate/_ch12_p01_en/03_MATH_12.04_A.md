The claim asks for the total number of possible samples of 5 items. This is equivalent to finding the number of ways to choose 5 items from a set of 20 items, without regard to order and without replacement. This is a combination problem, represented by $\binom{N}{n}$.

Using the values $N=20$ and $n=5$:

$$
\binom{20}{5} = \dfrac{20!}{5!(20-5)!}
$$

$$
\dfrac{20!}{5!(15)!} = \dfrac{20 \cdot 19 \cdot 18 \cdot 17 \cdot 16}{1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 4 \cdot 5}
$$

$$
20 \times 19 = 380
$$

$$
380 \times 18 = 6840
$$

$$
6840 \times 17 = 116280
$$

$$
116280 \times 16 = 1860480
$$

$$
\dfrac{1860480}{120} = 15504
$$

$$
\binom{20}{5} = 15504
$$

The total number of possible samples is $15\,504$. The statement claims this number is $15\,504$.

So the statement is True.
