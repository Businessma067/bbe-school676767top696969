This statement asks for the probability that all four digits in the PIN code are different, meaning no digit repeats. To calculate this probability, we first determine the number of PIN codes where all four digits are unique. This is a permutation problem, as the order of the digits matters and they must be distinct.

For the first digit, there are $10$ choices. For the second digit, there are $9$ remaining choices (it cannot be the same as the first). For the third digit, there are $8$ remaining choices, and for the fourth, there are $7$ remaining choices.

The number of PIN codes with all four digits different is:

$$
N_{\text{all different}} = P(10, 4) = 10 \times 9 \times 8 \times 7
$$

$$
10 \times 9 = 90
$$

$$
90 \times 8 = 720
$$

$$
720 \times 7 = 5040
$$

$$
N_{\text{all different}} = 5,040
$$

The probability of this event is the number of favorable outcomes divided by the total number of outcomes, $N_{\text{total}} = 10,000$, as established in the overview.

$$
P(\text{all different}) = \frac{N_{\text{all different}}}{N_{\text{total}}}
$$

$$
P(\text{all different}) = \frac{5,040}{10,000}
$$

$$
P(\text{all different}) = 0.504
$$

This probability is $50.4\%$. The claim states that this probability is greater than $55\%$.

So the statement is False.
