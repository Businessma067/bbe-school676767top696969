The claim asks for the probability that the sample contains no defective items. This corresponds to the hypergeometric probability for $P(X=0)$. We need to choose 0 defective items from the $K=4$ available, and $n-k = 5-0=5$ non-defective items from the $N-K=16$ available.

Using the general formula:

$$
P(X = k) = \frac{\binom{K}{k} \binom{N-K}{n-k}}{\binom{N}{n}}
$$

Substituting the values $N=20$, $K=4$, $n=5$, and $k=0$:

$$
P(X = 0) = \frac{\binom{4}{0} \binom{16}{5}}{\binom{20}{5}}
$$

We calculate the combinations:

$$
\binom{4}{0} = 1
$$

$$
\binom{16}{5} = \dfrac{16!}{5!(16-5)!}
$$

$$
\dfrac{16!}{5!(11)!} = \dfrac{16 \cdot 15 \cdot 14 \cdot 13 \cdot 12}{1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 4 \cdot 5}
$$

$$
16 \times 15 = 240
$$

$$
240 \times 14 = 3360
$$

$$
3360 \times 13 = 43680
$$

$$
43680 \times 12 = 524160
$$

$$
\dfrac{524160}{120} = 4368
$$

$$
\binom{16}{5} = 4368
$$

From the overview, the total number of samples is $\binom{20}{5} = 15\,504$. Now we substitute these values:

$$
P(X = 0) = \frac{1 \times 4\,368}{15\,504} = \frac{4\,368}{15\,504}
$$

$$
P(X = 0)

$$
\approx 0.2817
$$

This probability is approximately $28.17\%$. The statement claims this probability is less than $30\%$.

So the statement is True.
