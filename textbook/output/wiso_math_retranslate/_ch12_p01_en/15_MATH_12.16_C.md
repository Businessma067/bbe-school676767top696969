The claim asks for the probability that at least one of the other 7 people has the exact same birthday as one specific named person (say, the first person). This is equivalent to finding the probability that this specific person's birthday is shared by at least one other person in the group of 8.

It is easier to calculate the complement event: the probability that none of the other 7 people share the specific person's birthday. For any one of the other 7 people, the probability that their birthday is different from the specific person's birthday is $\frac{D-1}{D}$. Since the birthdays are independent, for $N-1$ other people, this probability is:

$$
P(\text{none of others share specific birthday}) = \left(\frac{D-1}{D}\right)^{N-1}
$$

For $D=365$ days and $N-1=7$ other people:

$$
P(\text{none of others share specific birthday}) = \left(\frac{365-1}{365}\right)^{7} = \left(\frac{364}{365}\right)^{7}
$$

$$
P(\text{none of others share specific birthday}) \approx 0.980979
$$

Now, we find the probability of the original event using the complement rule:

$$
P(\text{at least one other shares specific birthday}) = 1 - P(\text{none of others share specific birthday})
$$

$$
P(\text{at least one other shares specific birthday}) \approx 1 - 0.980979
$$

$$
P(\text{at least one other shares specific birthday}) \approx 0.019021
$$

Expressed as a percentage, this is approximately $1.9021\%$. The claim states this probability is less than $2\%$.
