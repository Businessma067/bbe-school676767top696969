The claim is about the probability of getting exactly one pair. To find this probability, we first need to count the number of ways to form an 'exactly one pair' hand. An 'exactly one pair' hand consists of two cards of one rank and three cards of three different ranks, none of which match the rank of the pair. This count, $N(\text{one pair})$, is determined by making a sequence of choices. First, one rank out of 13 available ranks is chosen for the pair. Next, two suits out of 4 are chosen for the cards of that rank. Then, three different ranks are chosen from the remaining 12 ranks for the three single cards, ensuring no three-of-a-kind or second pair is formed. Finally, one suit out of 4 is chosen for each of these 3 single cards.
