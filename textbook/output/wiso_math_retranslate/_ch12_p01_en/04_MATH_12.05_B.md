This statement asks for the probability that all five dice show different numbers. To calculate this, we first need to determine the number of favorable outcomes. For the first die, there are 6 possible outcomes. For the second die to be different from the first, there are 5 remaining outcomes. For the third die to be different from the first two, there are 4 remaining outcomes. This pattern continues until the fifth die.

The number of outcomes where all five dice show different numbers is the number of permutations of 6 items taken 5 at a time, denoted $P(6,5)$:

$$
P(6,5) = 6 \times 5 \times 4 \times 3 \times 2
$$

$$
6 \times 5 = 30
$$

$$
30 \times 4 = 120
$$

$$
120 \times 3 = 360
$$

$$
360 \times 2 = 720
$$

$$
P(6,5) = 720
$$

The total number of possible outcomes, as calculated in the overview, is $7776$. The probability of this event is the ratio of favorable outcomes to the total number of outcomes.

$$
P(\text{all five different}) = \frac{\text{Number of outcomes with all different numbers}}{\text{Total number of outcomes}}
$$

$$
P(\text{all five different}) = \frac{720}{7776}
$$

$$
P(\text{all five different}) \approx 0.0925926
$$

Converting this to a percentage, the probability is approximately $9.26\%$. The claim states that this probability is greater than $20\%$.

So the statement is False.
