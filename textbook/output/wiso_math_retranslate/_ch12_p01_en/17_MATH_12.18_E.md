This statement asks for the probability that all four digits in the PIN code are identical. This means the code consists of the same digit repeated four times (e.g., $0000, 1111, \ldots, 9999$).

There is one choice for the repeated digit (it can be any of $0, 1, \ldots, 9$). Once that digit is chosen, all four positions are filled with that same digit.

The number of PIN codes where all four digits are identical is:

$$
N_{\text{all identical}} = 10
$$

The probability of this event is the number of favorable outcomes divided by the total number of outcomes, $N_{\text{total}} = 10,000$.

$$
P(\text{all identical}) = \frac{N_{\text{all identical}}}{N_{\text{total}}}
$$

$$
P(\text{all identical}) = \frac{10}{10,000}
$$

$$
P(\text{all identical}) = \frac{1}{1,000}
$$

This probability is $0.001$. The claim states that this probability is greater than $1$ in $500$. To compare, we convert $1$ in $500$ to a decimal:

$$
\frac{1}{500} = 0.002
$$

.

So the statement is False.
