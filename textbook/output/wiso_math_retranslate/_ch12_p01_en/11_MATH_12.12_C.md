This claim asks for the number of ways to choose 3 digits from the $n$ available digits when the order does not matter. This is a combination problem, denoted by $C(n, k)$ or $\binom{n}{k}$.

From the solution overview and Statement A, we know that $n=8$. We need to calculate $C(8, 3)$. The combination formula is:

$$
C(n, k) = \binom{n}{k} = \frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}
$$

Substituting $n=8$ and $k=3$:

$$
C(8, 3) = \frac{8!}{3!(8-3)!}
$$

$$
C(8, 3) = \frac{8!}{3!5!}
$$

$$
C(8, 3) = \frac{8 \times 7 \times 6 \times 5!}{ (3 \times 2 \times 1) \times 5!}
$$

$$
C(8, 3) = \frac{8 \times 7 \times 6}{3 \times 2 \times 1}
$$

$$
C(8, 3) = 8 \times 7
$$

$$
8 \times 7 = 56
$$

$$
C(8, 3) = 56
$$

The number of ways to choose 3 unordered digits is $56$. The claim states that this number is $56$.

So the statement is True.
