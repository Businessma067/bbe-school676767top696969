$$
P(\text{at least one share with CEO}) = 1 - P(\text{none share with CEO})

P(\text{at least one share with CEO}) = 1 - 0.9703

=== EXPL 2 C ===
**C.** → False

This statement asks for the probability that all 12 employees have different birthdays. This is a direct application of the formula for the probability of all birthdays being distinct, which was calculated in the solution overview.

For $n$ employees and $N$ possible birthdays, the probability that all $n$ employees have different birthdays is:

$$
P(\text{all } n \text{ different}) = \frac{N \cdot (N-1) \cdot \ldots \cdot (N-n+1)}{N^n}
$$

Substituting $N=365$ and $n=12$, we find:

$$
P(\text{all 12 different}) = \frac{365 \cdot 364 \cdot \ldots \cdot 354}{365^{12}}
$$

$$
P(\text{all 12 different}) \approx 0.8330
$$

Expressed as a percentage, this is $83.30\%$. The claim states this probability is less than $80\%$.

So the statement is False.
