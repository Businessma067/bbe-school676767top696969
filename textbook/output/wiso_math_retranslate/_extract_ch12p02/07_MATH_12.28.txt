TITLE_DE Das Hochzeitsfoto
CONTEXT_DE Für ein Hochzeitsfoto stellen sich 3 Ehepaare (6 Personen) zufällig in einer Reihe auf.
STATEMENTS_DE:
  A. Die Gesamtzahl möglicher Aufstellungen beträgt 720.
  B. Die Wahrscheinlichkeit, dass alle drei Paare jeweils zusammenstehen, beträgt 1/15.
  C. Die Wahrscheinlichkeit, dass mindestens ein Paar zusammensteht, ist größer als 60%.
  D. Die Wahrscheinlichkeit, dass kein Paar zusammensteht, ist größer als 30%.
  E. Die Wahrscheinlichkeit, dass kein Paar zusammensteht, ist kleiner als die, dass alle drei Paare zusammenstehen.

=== EXPL 0 A ===
**A.** → True

The claim asks for the total number of possible lineups. This is the number of ways to arrange 6 distinct people in a row. The counting principle for arranging $n$ distinct items is $n!$.

For $n=6$ distinct people, the total number of arrangements is:

$$
N_{\text{total}} = 6!
$$

$$
N_{\text{total}} = 720
$$

Thus, the total number of possible lineups is 720.

So the statement is True.


=== EXPL 1 B ===
**B.** → True

This claim asks for the probability that all three couples end up standing together, where each couple occupies two adjacent positions. To calculate this, we first find the number of arrangements where this condition is met.

We can treat each couple as a single unit or 'block'. Since there are 3 couples, there are 3 such blocks. These 3 blocks can be arranged in $3!$ ways.

$$
N_{\text{blocks}} = 3! = 6
$$

Within each couple-block, the two members can swap positions. For example, if a couple consists of person X and person Y, they can be arranged as (X Y) or (Y X). This gives $2!$ arrangements for each couple. Since there are three couples, the total number of internal arrangements is $(2!)^3$.

$$
N_{\text{internal arrangements}} = (2!)^3 = (2)^3 = 8
$$

To find the total number of arrangements where all three couples stand together, we multiply the number of ways to arrange the blocks by the number of ways to arrange people within the blocks:

$$
N_{\text{all 3 couples together}} = N_{\text{blocks}} \times N_{\text{internal arrangements}}
$$

$$
N_{\text{all 3 couples together}} = 3! \times (2!)^3
$$

$$
N_{\text{all 3 couples together}} = 6 \times 8
$$

$$
6 \times 8 = 48
$$

$$
N_{\text{all 3 couples together}} = 48
$$

Now, we calculate the probability by dividing the number of favorable arrangements by the total number of possible lineups, $N_{\text{total}} = 720$, as found in the solution overview.

$$
P_{\text{all 3 couples together}} = \frac{N_{\text{all 3 couples together}}}{N_{\text{total}}}
$$

$$
P_{\text{all 3 couples together}} = \frac{48}{720}
$$

$$
P_{\text{all 3 couples together}} = \frac{1}{15}
$$

The probability is $1/15$.

So the statement is True.


=== EXPL 2 C ===
**C.** → True

This claim asks for the probability that at least one couple ends up standing together. This can be calculated using the Principle of Inclusion-Exclusion. Let $C_1, C_2, C_3$ be the events that couple 1, couple 2, and couple 3 stand together, respectively. We want to find $P(C_1 \cup C_2 \cup C_3)$, which requires calculating $N(C_1 \cup C_2 \cup C_3)$.

The Principle of Inclusion-Exclusion states:

$$
N(C_1 \cup C_2 \cup C_3) = \sum N(C_i) - \sum N(C_i \cap C_j) + N(C_1 \cap C_2 \cap C_3)
$$

First, consider the number of arrangements where a single specific couple, say $C_i$, stands together. Treat this couple as a single block. There are $6-2+1=5$ entities to arrange: the $C_i$ block and the 4 individual people from the other two couples. These 5 entities can be arranged in $5!$ ways. Within the $C_i$ block, the two members can be arranged in $2!$ ways.

$$
N(C_i) = 5! \times 2! = 120 \times 2
$$

$$
120 \times 2 = 240 = 240
$$

There are $\binom{3}{1} = 3$ such terms for single couples, so $\sum N(C_i) = 3 \times 240 = 720$.

Next, consider the number of arrangements where two specific couples, say $C_i$ and $C_j$, stand together. Treat each of these two couples as a block. There are $6-4+2=4$ entities to arrange: the $C_i$ block, the $C_j$ block, and the 2 individual people from the third couple. These 4 entities can be arranged in $4!$ ways. Each of the two couple blocks has $2!$ internal arrangements, so $(2!)^2$ for both.

$$
N(C_i \cap C_j) = 4! \times (2!)^2 = 24 \times 4
$$

$$
24 \times 4 = 96 = 96
$$

There are $\binom{3}{2} = 3$ such terms for pairs of couples, so $\sum N(C_i \cap C_j) = 3 \times 96 = 288$.

Finally, consider the number of arrangements where all three couples stand together. We calculate this by treating each couple as a block, arranging the 3 blocks in $3!$ ways, and allowing $2!$ arrangements within each of the 3 couples:

$$
N(C_1 \cap C_2 \cap C_3) = 3! \times (2!)^3 = 6 \times 8
$$

$$
6 \times 8 = 48 = 48
$$

Now, apply the Principle of Inclusion-Exclusion:

$$
N_{\text{at least one couple together}} = 720 - 288 + 48
$$

$$
720 - 288 = 432
$$

$$
432 + 48 = 480
$$

$$
N_{\text{at least one couple together}} = 480
$$

The probability is the number of favorable arrangements divided by the total number of possible lineups, $N_{\text{total}} = 720$.

$$
P_{\text{at least one couple together}} = \frac{N_{\text{at least one couple together}}}{N_{\text{total}}}
$$

$$
P_{\text{at least one couple together}} = \frac{480}{720}
$$

$$
P_{\text{at least one couple together}} = \frac{2}{3}
$$

As a decimal, this is approximately $0.6667$, or $66.67\%$. The claim states this probability is greater than $60\%$.

So the statement is True.


=== EXPL 3 D ===
**D.** → True

This claim asks for the probability that no couple ends up standing together. This event is the complement of 'at least one couple ends up standing together'.

The probability of an event's complement is $1$ minus the probability of the event itself. We have already determined that the probability of at least one couple standing together is $2/3$.

$$
P_{\text{no couple together}} = 1 - P_{\text{at least one couple together}}
$$

$$
P_{\text{no couple together}} = 1 - \frac{2}{3}
$$

$$
P_{\text{no couple together}} = \frac{1}{3}
$$

As a decimal, this is approximately $0.3333$, or $33.33\%$. The claim states this probability is greater than $30\%$.

So the statement is True.


=== EXPL 4 E ===
**E.** → False

This claim compares two probabilities: the probability that no couple ends up standing together, and the probability that all three couples end up standing together. We will use the values derived earlier in the solution.

The probability that no couple ends up standing together is $1/3$.

$$
P_{\text{no couple together}} = \frac{1}{3}
$$

The probability that all three couples end up standing together is $1/15$.

$$
P_{\text{all 3 couples together}} = \frac{1}{15}
$$

To compare these values, we can express them with a common denominator:

$$
P_{\text{no couple together}} = \frac{1}{3} = \frac{5}{15}
$$

Comparing the two probabilities:

$$
P_{\text{no couple together}} = \frac{5}{15}
$$

Since $\frac{5}{15} > \frac{1}{15}$, it means that $P_{\text{no couple together}}$ is greater than $P_{\text{all 3 couples together}}$. The claim states that the probability that no couple ends up standing together is *less than* the probability that all three couples end up standing together.

So the statement is False.
