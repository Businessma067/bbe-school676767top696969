TITLE_DE Kombinatorische Wahrscheinlichkeit 40
CONTEXT_DE Ein Ausschuss von 4 wird zufällig aus 12 Personen gewählt (5 Frauen, 7 Männer).
STATEMENTS_DE:
  A. Die Wahrscheinlichkeit für genau 2 Frauen und 2 Männer ist größer als 42%.
  B. Die Wahrscheinlichkeit für mindestens eine Frau ist gleich 1 minus der Wahrscheinlichkeit eines rein männlichen Ausschusses.
  C. Die Wahrscheinlichkeit, dass eine bestimmte Person gewählt wird, beträgt 4/12.
  D. Die Wahrscheinlichkeit eines rein männlichen Ausschusses ist kleiner als 7%.
  E. Die Wahrscheinlichkeit für genau 2 Frauen und 2 Männer ist mehr als 6-mal so groß wie die eines rein männlichen Ausschusses.

=== EXPL 0 A ===
**A.** → True

This statement asks for the probability that the committee has exactly 2 women and 2 men. To find this, we first calculate the number of ways to choose 2 women from the 5 available women and 2 men from the 7 available men. Then we divide this by the total number of possible committees.

The number of ways to choose 2 women from 5 is:

$$
\binom{12}{4} = \dfrac{12!}{4!(12-4)!}
$$

$$
\dfrac{12!}{4!(8)!} = \dfrac{12 \cdot 11 \cdot 10 \cdot 9}{1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 4}
$$

$$
12 \times 11 = 132
$$

$$
132 \times 10 = 1320
$$

$$
1320 \times 9 = 11880
$$

$$
\dfrac{11880}{24} = 495
$$

$$
\binom{12}{4} = 495
$$

$$
\binom{5}{2} = \dfrac{5!}{2!(5-2)!}
$$

$$
\dfrac{5!}{2!(3)!} = \dfrac{5 \cdot 4}{1 \cdot 2}
$$

$$
5 \times 4 = 20
$$

$$
\dfrac{20}{2} = 10
$$

$$
\binom{5}{2} = 10
$$

The number of ways to choose 2 men from 7 is:

$$
\binom{7}{2} = \dfrac{7!}{2!(7-2)!}
$$

$$
\dfrac{7!}{2!(5)!} = \dfrac{7 \cdot 6}{1 \cdot 2}
$$

$$
7 \times 6 = 42
$$

$$
\dfrac{42}{2} = 21
$$

$$
\binom{7}{2} = 21
$$

The number of committees with exactly 2 women and 2 men is the product of these two numbers:

$$
N_{\text{2 women, 2 men}} = \binom{5}{2} \times \binom{7}{2} = 10 \times 21
$$

$$
10 \times 21 = 210 = 210
$$

The probability of such a committee composition is this number divided by the total number of committees, which is $495$ from the overview:

$$
P(\text{2 women, 2 men}) = \frac{210}{495}
$$

$$
P(\text{2 women, 2 men}) \approx 0.424242
$$

Expressed as a percentage, this is approximately $42.42\%$. The claim states this probability is greater than $42\%$.

So the statement is True.


=== EXPL 1 B ===
**B.** → True

This statement relates the probability that the committee includes at least one woman to the probability that the committee is entirely men. The event 'at least one woman' is the complement of the event 'no women'. If a committee has no women, it must be composed entirely of men, since all committee members must be selected from the group of 12 people comprising men and women.

According to the rule of complementary events, for any event $E$, the probability of $E$ occurring is $P(E) = 1 - P(E^c)$, where $E^c$ is the complement of $E$. In this case, if $E$ is the event 'at least one woman', then $E^c$ is the event 'no women'.

Therefore, the probability that the committee includes at least one woman is $1$ minus the probability that it includes no women, which means it is composed entirely of men.

$$
P(\text{at least one woman}) = 1 - P(\text{no women})
$$

$$
P(\text{at least one woman}) = 1 - P(\text{entirely men})
$$

This relation is a fundamental principle of probability.

So the statement is True.

$$
P(\text{2 women, 2 men}) = \frac{210}{495}

$$
P(\text{entirely men}) = \frac{35}{495}

$$
\frac{P(\text{2 women, 2 men})}{P(\text{entirely men})} = \dfrac{210}{495}\cdot\dfrac{495}{35}=\dfrac{210}{35}

\frac{P(\text{2 women, 2 men})}{P(\text{entirely men})} = 6
